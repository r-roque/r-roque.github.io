Overview

A platform for Hispanic journalists at UNC — built from a logo and a color palette

Lo Nuestro is the digital platform of NAHJ at UNC-Chapel Hill — the National Association of Hispanic Journalists chapter at UNC. The mission is to give Hispanic students a dedicated space to practice journalism, publish their work, and build a community that is underrepresented in mainstream campus media.

I was brought on as the lead designer with two things to work from: the Lo Nuestro logo and a color palette. Everything else — the layout system, typographic hierarchy, page architecture, responsive behavior, and visual personality — was designed from scratch.

The Mission

"A home for Hispanic journalists and storytellers at UNC — where representation in media starts with building the platform yourself."

🎨

What I was given

The Lo Nuestro logo and a color palette — navy, pink, blue, and white. Every layout decision, typographic choice, component design, page structure, and responsive system was built from those two starting points.

The Design Challenge

Building a credible news platform that also feels like home

The hardest thing about designing Lo Nuestro wasn't the layouts — it was the tone. A news platform needs to feel credible, structured, and journalistic. But Lo Nuestro also needed to feel welcoming, culturally grounded, and distinctly rooted in Hispanic journalism and identity. Those two things can pull in opposite directions if you're not careful.

The solution was to let the structure be editorial (clean grid, clear hierarchy, proper article formatting) and let the brand elements carry the personality — the LN monogram, the warm navy palette, the pink accent that runs through every interactive element. Professional infrastructure, cultural soul.

Design Process

From blank canvas to full design system

01

Brand Interpretation

Translated the logo and color palette into a full design language — selecting Libre Baskerville for editorial credibility, establishing the navy/pink/blue system, and defining how the LN monogram would anchor every page's footer.

02

Information Architecture

Designed the full site structure: Latest Posts, Magazine Archive, Categories, About (Purpose, Why Latino?, Meet the Team), Issues, and Contact. Each section was designed to serve both the reader experience and the student journalist/contributor community.

03

Wireframing — Three Homepage Iterations

Developed three distinct homepage layouts before landing on the final direction — exploring different article grid densities, nav treatments, and feature story hierarchies. Each iteration was refined based on team feedback before moving to hi-fi.

04

High-Fidelity Design System

Built every page in full fidelity — Home, Article Post, Categories, About, and Issues — plus a complete responsive mobile system with multiple nav menu iterations and full mobile layouts for every key page.

05

Responsive Design

Designed the full mobile experience alongside desktop — nav menus, article layouts, category pages, the About section with team photos, and the Issues "Coming Soon" state. Every desktop page has a corresponding mobile view.

Iteration

Three homepage directions before the right one

The wireframing phase produced three distinct homepage layouts — each exploring different content hierarchies, article grid structures, and how to balance the "latest posts" feed with featured editorial content. The final direction landed on a clean two-column grid that gives featured stories room to breathe while maintaining scan-ability for the latest posts.

Pages Designed

A complete five-page editorial platform

Lo Nuestro was designed as a full platform — not just a homepage concept. Every core page was built out in high fidelity, giving the development team a complete, handoff-ready design system to build from.

Responsive Design

Every page, every screen size

Mobile wasn't an afterthought — it was designed in parallel with desktop. The mobile system includes multiple nav menu iterations (exploring different information depths), full article and category layouts, team photo grids on the About page, and a "Coming Soon" state for the Issues page. The result is a design system the dev team can build responsively from day one.

Purpose-Driven Design

Outcomes & Status

Designs complete. Platform in development.

The full design system for Lo Nuestro is complete and has been handed off to the development team. The platform is currently being built and will launch as the primary digital publication for NAHJ at UNC — a news and culture destination for Hispanic journalists and storytellers at UNC Chapel Hill.

This project represents the most complete design system in my portfolio — full responsive coverage, five distinct page types, three wireframe iterations, multiple nav explorations, and a visual language built entirely from a logo and a color palette.
