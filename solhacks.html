01Context

Year one shipped fast, desktop-only, and without a single design owner.

SolHacks launched in 2025 as UNC SHPE’s first hackathon. As Marketing Chair, I built the brand and website from scratch, but design ownership was split across the team, and the site shipped desktop-only due to time constraints.

Moving into Director of External Marketing and then Executive Director, I took on full design ownership of the event, brand systems, and the site itself.

02Problem

Grow the event without it depending on one person.

Year one drew 66 attendees and 16 projects, on a site that didn’t work on phones and an organization that ran on a few people’s memory. My mandate was to grow attendance and partnerships while professionalizing how the organization worked.

How might we grow attendance and partnerships with a brand, site, and team that outlast any one organizer?

03Research

Year one was the research.

2025’s identity work meant matching the site’s palette to our existing club logo under a tight pre-event timeline. There was no room for research or testing, just what was necessary to ship. That made the first event our best evidence: the clearest gap was that the site had no mobile support at all.

For 2026, our Executive Director handed off a newly designated SolHacks logo and gave me real room to explore: night gradients, day gradients, solid fields, and a desert/sandy-day palette, a dozen-plus directions before landing on the final system.

04Solution

A warm, responsive site built on real components.

2026 Responsive Site 2025 Desktop-Only Site

The 2026 site adds the mobile-responsive views the first version lacked, on a warm desert palette that matches the new logo.

My own Figma fluency grew alongside the brand. 2025 was a flat, single-page prototype. By 2026 I was building with real components and variants, then wiring the whole thing into an interactive, prototyped flow instead of static screens.

Direction

My first instinct for the 2026 hero was a cooler blue-and-orange complementary palette. Our Executive Director wanted to stay true to a warm-dominant identity instead, so I moved the exploration in that direction. The desert and sandy-day palettes are the result of that call, not my original one, and it’s the direction that actually shipped.

Ownership

I was the only person on the team with real UX training, which meant a lot of my role was saying no with reasoning attached.

I turned down a four-CTA hero and a request to fit all of the event’s information onto a single path, both in favor of clearer hierarchy and dedicated pages. These were decisions I had to defend to a non-designer leadership team.

Outcome

  • Attendance grew from 66 to 150+, on a site that now works on phones.
  • Restructured nine committee roles and built a master semester timeline so the org didn’t run on one person’s memory.
  • Recruited a dedicated Director of UX role to carry the system forward after graduation.
  • Expanded outreach, landing partnerships with Microsoft, IBM, NetApp, Wells Fargo, Fidelity, and more.

05Scenarios

Who the 2026 site was built for.

  • A student finds the event on their phone

    In 2025 the site didn’t work on mobile at all.

    In 2026 they get responsive views built for that screen.

  • A first-timer deciding whether to go

    Instead of four competing buttons and one long page, the hero has a clear hierarchy.

    Details live on dedicated pages they can reach on purpose.

  • The next Director of UX

    They inherit a Figma file built on real components and variants, not flat screens.

    The brand can keep going without me in the room.

06Learnings

The real test is whether it holds without me.

Being the only person on the team who could speak to UX meant learning to advocate for those decisions in terms a non-designer leadership team would accept. It was my first time communicating design at this scale, and it’s a skill that matters as much on large projects as the design work itself.

Heading into 2027 as Executive Director, the real test isn’t the 2026 numbers, it’s whether the system holds without me in the room. The committee restructure and the dedicated Director of UX role were built specifically so continuity doesn’t depend on any one person, including me.
